ARAP analysis

Interpretation of identified primary sources

Policy

The July 8, 2026 General Order 325 explicitly permits authorized users to create custom hotlists (IV.A.4, p.2). It specifies names containing the user’s full name and rank, one-hotlist guidance, written supervisor approval for additional lists, and monthly audits that purge lists outside the naming convention. Manual entries must be removed when investigative relevance or legitimate law-enforcement interest ends (IV.B.4.c, p.2).

The policy limits server access to criminal-justice reasons by authorized employees, requires an apparent search reason where the query page has a space, and calls for quarterly audits (IV.D.2-4, p.3). General server retention is no longer than 150 days, with specified case-file preservation exceptions (IV.D.1, p.3). Visually reviewed revision marks change monthly reporting to biannual reporting. The policy describes January-June and July-December reports, 18-month preservation, scan totals, list names and three match-outcome counts (IV.D.6-8, pp.3-4). These are summaries of this policy’s text, not conclusions about statutory compliance.

The copy replaces a July 14, 2025 version. That earlier policy is absent. The July 3 Q2 audit predates the revised policy; this library cannot establish which custom-list rules applied to every earlier search.

Audit

The released July 3, 2026 Q2 audit memorandum (LR-015, p.1) says the review focused on whether documented reasons for LPR searches during the previous three months were consistent with department policy. Its compliance statement concerns documentation of search reasons. A paragraph is withheld with a general “25-19-105” label. The released page supplies no underlying search logs, review sample size, selection criteria or independent case verification.

The released paragraph does not establish that every search had an authorized factual purpose. It also does not identify the officer associated with reported repeated searches or establish a disciplinary result. ARAP’s October 7 request (LR-030, pp.1-2) seeks those supporting records. Request confirmation demonstrates the request was submitted; it does not authenticate the reported facts.

LR-023 p.2 shows 79,996 official hotlist alerts, 223,952 “Your Custom Hotlist Alerts,” and 46,263 “Other Custom Hotlist Alerts on Your Networks”; these sum to 350,211. The two custom categories sum to 270,215, an ARAP arithmetic calculation of displayed alert categories. This is not a count of custom lists, searches, unique people, unauthorized alerts or misconduct. The categories do not establish the factual purpose of each list or alert.

LR-023 p.3 shows 91 total successes, 86 cases cleared, 54 recovered stolen vehicles and $856,299 recovered property. The report describes outcomes differently from the dashboard labels; the three per-list counts described in the policy are not clearly traceable through these charts. LR-026 p.3 includes January-December 2025 bars even though the report’s first page purports to cover the first half. ARAP asked about these issues in its August 20 email (LR-022); no answer is in the supplied folders.

Operational RTCC metrics include multiple technologies and activities. March/April/May dashboards report 11/11/19 successes (LR-003/004/006 p.2); June reports 16 (LR-018 p.6); Q2 reports 47 (LR-018 p.3). The RTCC semiannual dashboard reports 90 successes (LR-017 p.2), while the separate six-month ALPR report reports 91. These may reflect different snapshots or definitions. Do not treat RTCC arrests, selected success narratives or dashboard successes as counts of prosecutions.
