AUD-2026-001 • New August 12, 2026

Sherwood ALPR Audit Evaluation

The new evaluation reviews 1,502 displayed user-audit IDs for May through July 6, three network-inclusion exports, the July 11–August 9 public-portal analysis, and Policy 11.08.00.

The May and June network exports each contain 1,048,575 data rows—the maximum number available below one Excel header row—so completeness cannot be assumed. A network-inclusion row means an outside query included Sherwood's network; it does not prove that Sherwood returned, viewed, or used a result.

Open verification question. No six-month statistical report or annual audit workpapers were identified in the selected record set. That means they were not available for this review, not that they do not exist.

Evidence update • Version 3.5

What the latest records show—and what they do not

The public audit covers July 11 through August 9, 2026. Its 185 rows contain a timestamp, a field labeled networkCount, and the query justification “Investigation.” The export does not provide a case number, offense type, queried plate, named agency, state, network, or database.

Five rows occurred within 60 seconds of the immediately preceding row. Those events form four short clusters involving nine records. Close timing can reflect legitimate follow-up work, correction, or repetition; timing alone does not establish misuse.

Responsible inference limit. A network count is not a list of agencies or databases. The export cannot establish which states were reached. It supports a narrower public question: what incident, case, or investigative records explain the searches—particularly the 104 reporting at least 1,000 networks and the 21 reporting between 6,248 and 6,295?

Four evidence questions now supported by source records

H-066 • H-078

Who is the public-record custodian?

The CITY-2026-130 email production documents advice to amend the agreement so Flock would be designated custodian and captured-record requests would be directed to Flock. The record does not establish that Sherwood adopted the advice or that it has legal effect.

Read H-066 · Read the H-078 review

H-080 • H-081

Which terms were actually preserved?

The 2022 contract production includes an attached Government Agency Agreement. The later five-page order form—signed by the City on December 22, 2023 and by Flock on February 19, 2024—incorporates terms through a web address but does not attach or identify a dated copy.

This comparison does not prove that any operative term changed. It asks whether the City preserved the version incorporated when the order was executed.

H-082

“Hate us anymore” and possible future coverage

In a February 5, 2026 installation exchange, Flock’s Kevin Cutler wrote, “I just don’t want your Chief to hate us anymore!!” and said he hoped the experience would not discourage “future coverage to fill in the gaps.”

The wording acknowledges concern about the department’s experience while anticipating possible additional coverage. It does not establish that Sherwood approved an expansion.

Read the one-page redacted excerpt

H-083

Thirty days, 150 days, and a separate archive

The May 2026 email chain records a Sherwood official seeking access beyond 30 days, stating that Arkansas law required retention, and proposing a monthly download-and-archive process. Flock quoted $15,000 per year for Traffic Analytics; the same exchange discusses a possible discount and dissatisfaction with cost and installation.

A portal statement of 30 days and a separately maintained archive can both be true. These emails do not establish what Sherwood actually retained, where it was stored, or which legal rule controlled. Those questions require policy, export, deletion, and archive records.

Arkansas network-page preservation

ARAP’s July 10 research notes recorded a Flock webpage displaying “63 customer networks in Arkansas.” No preserved capture of that number has been located, so it is treated as a historical, presently unverified observation—not a proven statewide total.

On August 10, the live Arkansas network URL returned a 404 page, and the Wayback Machine reported that the URL had been excluded. The screenshots establish those responses at the time captured; they do not establish when the page changed, who requested an archive exclusion, or why.
