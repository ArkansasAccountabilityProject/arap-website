# Four Sherwood FOIA requests: records, responses and unresolved questions

Arkansas Accountability Project | October 23, 2026

The selected records document substantial disclosure, later additions, changes to redactions, and unresolved questions about attachment and search coverage. They support specific follow-up inquiries, not a citywide compliance score or a finding of intentional withholding.

## What this review can establish

The reviewed records show meaningful production and later additions, together with unresolved category, attachment, redaction and cross-request reconciliation questions. They do not establish that Sherwood never provided records that appear in another request, that every City office was or was not searched, or that officials intentionally withheld or destroyed records.

This is a purposively selected set of four requests, not a representative sample of all Sherwood FOIA responses. Three concern overlapping drone-program records. Count no compliance percentage, misconduct rate or independent incident total from these cases. Historical portal statuses, requester statements and download dates are identified separately. No live portal inventory or exhaustive search of every email account is claimed.

## Arkansas response requirements

Arkansas Code § 25-19-105(a)(3)-(4) requires a written response to a written request stating when responsive records do not exist, identifying applicable exemptions for existing responsive records withheld, or identifying the appropriate custodian when administrative control rests elsewhere, if known or readily ascertainable. Email is permitted. The cited provision does not expressly require an individual-file withholding index or a detailed search narrative. Subsection (f) requires segregable nonexempt portions and an indication of deleted information. Subsection (d)(2) addresses readily available/convertible electronic formats and does not require creation of new records. These rules do not by themselves resolve whether any selected response violated the law. [Statutory text](https://law.justia.com/codes/arkansas/title-25/chapter-19/section-25-19-105/).

Captured plate data has additional privacy restrictions and exceptions under [§ 12-12-1808](https://law.justia.com/codes/arkansas/title-12/subtitle-2/chapter-12/subchapter-18/section-12-12-1808/). CITY-112 raises a disclosure-authority question; this review does not determine that the release violated that section.

## CITY-2026-112: Vehicle-specific ALPR captures and disclosure handling

**Submitted:** June 30, 2026

**Requested:** ALPR captures, images, timestamps and locations for one specified Arkansas vehicle, June 1-July 1, 2026. The request asked for expedition because of the stated purge period and for justification if records were denied or redacted. The plate is withheld from this publication.

**Status evidence:** July 14 portal snapshot: Completed. No present live-portal status is asserted.

**Produced:** The July 1-labelled ZIP contains 85 files: 84 JPEGs and one CSV with 80 nonempty data rows, spanning 19 capture dates and 12 cameras. ZIP labels are download labels, not proof of delivery time.

**Assessment:** Substantial responsive image and structured-data production is demonstrated. All 80 CSV image-reference groups are represented after normalizing punctuation, milliseconds and numbered filename suffixes. The four additional JPEGs have different byte hashes; they are not established duplicates. The original disclosure authority and handling safeguards remain unresolved in the selected response materials.

**Response history:** ARAP's July 14 clarification sought disclosure authority, verification, approval and preservation. The preserved email includes a delivery failure for one copied address. It does not establish delivery to every recipient or nonresponse by the whole City. Later screenshots repeat the inquiry, not a substantive City reply.

### Findings

1. 85 is the file count, not the count of detections or photographs. The earlier clarification's approximate 85-image/detection description should be corrected to 84 JPEGs and 80 CSV rows.
2. The 80 rows have 80 distinct exact date/time/camera combinations. The existing website's 74-event analysis applies a separate 60-second same-camera burst-collapse rule. It is a derived grouping, not the agency's raw count or proof that six rows are exact duplicates.
3. The July 14 letter says the requester did not assert vehicle ownership or consent and asks what authority supported release. That is the requester's statement, not proof that no other authorization existed. The original request itself does not assert ownership or consent.
4. This case concerns disclosure handling as well as completeness. Producing more identifying information is not inherently a better FOIA response. The public materials here omit the plate and do not republish the vehicle photographs.

## CITY-2026-150: Drone acquisition, integration, policies and authority

**Submitted:** August 18, 2026

**Requested:** Nine categories concerning acquisition/financial documents; identifying equipment and funding; subscriptions; integration; communications; policies; third-party access; FAA authority; and training. Communications/development period begins January 1, 2025 and runs through processing. Native format is requested when reasonably available.

**Status evidence:** August 18 portal snapshot: Submitted. August 21 City email says work continued and promises another update by Monday afternoon. These are historical snapshots, not proof of current status or completion date.

**Produced:** August 26-labelled ZIP: 24 files (21 PDFs, two DOCX, one TXT). October 5-labelled ZIP: 58 files (50 PDFs, five MSG, two DOCX, one TXT). The later archive retains all 24 initial filenames: 18 byte-identical, six byte-changed, plus 34 added filenames. The extracted August-labelled folder is not an accurate snapshot of the 24-file ZIP.

**Assessment:** The archive comparison credits additional disclosure: contracts/quotes, policies, FAA material, installation, integration and communications are present. It also identifies new obscuring in replacement versions. Filename/date and file-count changes must not be misreported as a count of new emails, unique records or deliberate omissions.

**Response history:** The August 21 status email is affirmative evidence the City communicated ongoing work. Both dated archives are preserved. The selected records do not establish why the batch changed, when each item was uploaded, whether the 58-file archive was final, or the City’s field-specific rationale for added obscuring.

### Findings

1. The later archive adds 34 filenames, removes none, changes six and retains 18 byte-for-byte. Six changed files are five quote/proposal PDFs and Drone Emails 2-Redacted.pdf. Both versions of each changed file retain the same page count.
2. In each of five quote/proposal PDFs, pages 7-10 render fully black in the later archive where the earlier versions displayed BRINC product material. These are 20 page instances, not 20 distinct underlying documents. Page 11 also changes in each file; the inspected example obscures a brochure photograph. The reason and legal basis are not established by the comparison.
3. Drone Emails 2 differs visually on pages 22, 23, 31, 33, 36 and 39. Examples include obscuring an equipment line and resource URLs. Do not republish previously exposed resource links or infer a motive from the later treatment.
4. The later archive contains native MSG files, so a statement that Sherwood produced only scanned PDFs would be inaccurate. Many substantial email compilations are image-based PDFs. Their searchability and attachment completeness remain separate questions.
5. The signed two-page agreement incorporates a Motorola Customer Agreement by reference. Two separately downloaded October 20 web versions of that agreement are research references, not CITY-2026-150 production files or proof of the controlling terms at signing.
6. Two native emails in this archive concern the handling of CITY-2026-164. They are cross-request evidence, not part of that nine-file ZIP. The routing email also records a staff assertion that the requester waived three business days; this review has not located the underlying agreement and does not adopt that assertion as independently verified.

### Findings

1. The original request expressly reaches City personnel regardless of department. Its subject is Citywide even though records were handled through police. A police response alone does not establish the City failed to search other offices.
