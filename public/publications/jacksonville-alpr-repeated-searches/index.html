Jacksonville ALPR: repeated searches and mobile use

The audit records identify concentrated search activity worth reconciling with incident records. They do not establish misconduct.

The strongest comparison

Account Account 08 made 24 lookups of Plate A across 9 days, 22 hours, 42 minutes and 39 seconds, on three dates in December 2025. Every reason was a capitalization variant of “stolen.” All case fields were blank. Scope expanded from two networks to 269 and eventually 5,657.

Across accounts, Plate A appears 63 times, including 61 entries within a rolling 240-hour window involving seven account labels. Those 61 entries are not one account’s activity. Two accounts supplied different report references. That offers a concrete investigative explanation to check against incident and stolen-vehicle recovery records.

The reported Little Rock benchmark supplied to ARAP is 28 searches of one plate over 10 days, classified by LRPD as misuse. Its original determination has not been independently verified for this publication. No same-displayed-account / explicit-plate group in this production reaches 28 entries within a rolling 240-hour window. A search count is not a misconduct threshold.

Mobile bursts and changes in documentation

Plate B has 23 mobile lookups in 22 minutes, 45 seconds, all using an assault/battery reason, blank case fields and 159 networks. Plate C has 15 mobile lookups over 8 hours, 9 minutes, 53 seconds using a theft reason and scopes of three or 159 networks. Their query periods change. Incident reconciliation and vendor definitions of refreshes, retries and search events are needed before interpreting these bursts.

Plate H has nine mobile lookups by one account using Traffic Infraction, over 10 days plus 14 seconds. Only eight fit a strict 240-hour window. Another account searched the string twice later using a fake-registration reason. This substantive reason difference across accounts warrants reconciliation, not an assumption of fabricated justification.

For Plate F, one account entered the same report reference on its first eight lookups and left it blank on the final three. For Plate I, seven lookups used the generic reason “search.” For Plate M, six explicit-plate mobile lookups used Drugs and the case-field value “1,” whose meaning has not been established. An additional filter-only search is associated with that string but excluded from the explicit-plate counts.

What Jacksonville’s policy requires

Policy 10-28, effective November 11, 2024, does not expressly require a case number for every ALPR search. It restricts use to official agency and public-safety purposes. Page 2, section III.E calls for documenting a reason for manual plate entry in its hotlist context; that is not a universal case-number requirement.

Blank case fields are visible documentation gaps, not established policy violations or proof that no supporting case existed. A separate directive, newer policy or platform setting might impose additional requirements. The applicable records remain a follow-up question.

The policy’s “Mobile LPR system” definition refers to vehicle-mounted cameras. It should not be equated with the Mobile labels in these audit exports. The September response states that no policy addresses mobile use specifically and that no mobile deployment records were located. It answers “No Data” to the mobile oversight category. Those statements do not prove that no oversight occurred.

Coverage and inference limits

These findings come from nine native audit CSVs in the September 26 production, labeled December 2025 through August 2026. They contain 631 distinct IDs: 435 entries with explicit plate strings and 196 without them. December mixes mobile and ordinary searches. Every January–August entry is labeled Mobile. Monthly totals do not measure all Jacksonville usage.

The separate June 14–July 14 PDF has 267 IDs, but its License Plate column is blank throughout and its query intervals are clipped. It cannot independently establish same-plate repetition. Seventeen IDs overlap the September files, matching all 17 PDF entries labeled Mobile. The two productions must not simply be added together.

Clusters group the exact displayed account label (ignoring letter case) and explicit plate string. Account aliases, partial targets and ambiguous plate characters are not merged. The 86 groups include 28 with at least five entries, nine with at least ten and two with at least twenty. Different states can share a plate string; the grouping does not establish the same vehicle or owner.

Distinct audit IDs establish separate logged events, not necessarily separate deliberate tracking decisions. The logs do not establish successful matches, vehicle ownership, duty status, personal relationships, disclosure of results or an agency finding of misuse. Nighttime activity is not proof of unauthorized off-duty use.

Questions for follow-up records

  1. Which incident, CAD, report or investigative assignment supports each cluster?
  2. For Plate A, what is the relationship between the two recorded report references, and when was the vehicle recovered or the matter closed?
  3. Do mobile refreshes or retries generate separate search IDs?
  4. What explains changing network scope, reasons and missing case references?
  5. What existing supervisory reviews, mobile permissions and duty-status records apply?
  6. What export filters explain the missing ordinary-search coverage after December?
