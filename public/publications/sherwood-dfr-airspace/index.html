Sherwood's Drone Response Area: What the FAA Maps and Waiver Show

The city boundary, the FAA grid, the proposed response circle and an operating authorization answer different questions.

What was checked

Actual city and FAA geometries, the BRINC Talking Paper, site and installation records, and all six pages of Sherwood's produced FAA certificate.

Standard: Facts First. Questions Second. Conclusions Last.

How much of Sherwood is affected by controlled airspace? Using the city's authoritative municipal polygon and current FAA feature data, ARAP calculates that 65.2% of Sherwood's estimated land area lies inside published surface-controlled-airspace footprints. FAA UAS Facility Map cells cover a larger share: 75.7%. Treating those two footprints as interchangeable would overstate mapped surface coverage by about 2.182 square miles.

The city polygon totals 21.155 square miles. Subtracting 0.410 square miles of Census mapped water yields a land denominator of 20.744 square miles. The 85 intersecting UASFM cells include 69 attributed to Little Rock AFB (LRF) and 16 to Little Rock (LIT). There are 27 zero-ceiling cells and 58 400-foot cells. Attribution checks all five airport fields; no positive-area airport overlap occurs inside the city in this snapshot. Unions prevent double counting.

Independently measured surface coverage is 12.197 square miles for LRF (58.8%) and 1.327 square miles for LIT (6.4%). LRF Class D and surface Class E2 share a footprint and are counted once; activation depends on published schedules and NOTAMs. Elevated Class C shelves and Class E5 polygons beginning at 700 or 1,200 feet above ground are excluded from surface coverage. No ORK-attributed cells or ORK surface polygons intersect Sherwood in the queried layers. That absence does not remove the need to avoid airport traffic or check other restrictions.

The records support a dock site at police headquarters

The April 14 BRINC site survey identifies a roof station at Police Headquarters, 2201 E Kiehl Avenue, with coordinates 34.833337827083945, -92.208861322158. The signed August 19 installation summary identifies the installed site at headquarters and the same address. The map therefore labels a documented SPD / BRINC dock site. The pin uses the survey coordinate; final as-built coordinates were not independently measured.

There is a precise-location issue to resolve before operational configuration. The survey describes the site as Class D. Current FAA geometry places the surveyed coordinate roughly 645 metres outside the nearest surface footprint and essentially on a UASFM edge. Address geocoding moves the point about 29 metres east, into a 400-foot LRF cell. A regional map should not settle a launch-point authorization decision; reconcile the final coordinate, current charts and NOTAMs.

The three-mile circle does not reproduce the Talking Paper's land estimate

The BRINC Talking Paper describes a roughly three-mile response radius and estimates 90–95% city coverage. Using a three-statute-mile geodesic circle around the survey coordinate and the current city boundary produces 15.616 square miles, or 75.3% of city land. That is an area comparison, not a measure of residents served, calls covered, response time or operational availability. The basis of the Talking Paper's larger estimate needs clarification.

The circle is a proposed planning illustration. It is not an FAA approval boundary or a three-mile restriction in the produced certificate. Across the whole 28.273-square-mile circle, including water and neighboring jurisdictions, 54.0% intersects surface-airspace footprints and 66.5% intersects UASFM cells. Those whole-circle percentages use a different denominator from the city-land figures.

Sherwood has a produced FAA waiver — with conditions

It would be misleading to suggest that Sherwood has no BVLOS authority. The produced FAA Part 91 Certificate of Waiver and Authorization 91.113-2026-00585 was signed May 14, 2026 and lists an effective term of May 13, 2026 through May 31, 2030, subject to cancellation. Page 2 also makes it an airspace authorization under its special provisions, excluding prohibited or restricted airspace. The production alone does not establish later amendments, cancellation status or actual flight compliance.

For controlled-airspace BVLOS, page 3 limits flights to at or below 200 feet AGL or below the UASFM altitude, whichever is lower. A 400-foot grid does not raise that routine BVLOS limit to 400 feet. A zero-foot grid does not provide positive-altitude access under those blanket provisions. The obstacle-related alternative applies in Class G and is capped at 400 feet; it is not a general 300-foot allowance everywhere.

Provision 12 requires additional CAPS/CADZ airspace authority above grid heights, in controlled airspace where UASFMs are inapplicable, and in other listed cases. The Talking Paper describes exceeding grid values through tower communication. The certificate does not establish that a call alone substitutes for the written additional authority it requires. For emergency departures from the approved envelope, provision 32 addresses Special Government Interest approval.

What this means for automated launches

The Talking Paper describes optional rules that could launch a drone from CAD priority calls, Flock hits or officer-duress signals. Those descriptions establish capabilities or plans; they do not establish that those rules are enabled, used, or approved for unattended launches.

An automated workflow must fit the actual authority and its retained remote-pilot responsibilities. The certificate includes current-grid review, outbound and lost-link return-route altitude limits, air-traffic awareness and deconfliction, and specific ADS-B detection arrangements during BVLOS. Its preflight checklist provision includes a critical/urgent emergency-management qualification. The incorporated FAA application should also be reviewed; a complete copy has not been identified in this production.

LAANC answers a different question. It can provide qualifying airspace authorization for ordinary Part 107 operations at participating facilities; it does not approve BVLOS. Current LRF/LIT grid attributes flag LAANC participation, but they do not show that Sherwood's Part 91 missions use LAANC. FAA directs Part 107 waiver users seeking controlled-airspace access to FAADroneZone. Sherwood's produced Part 91 certificate identifies CAPS/CADZ for additional-authority cases. A Part 107 pilot credential does not itself make a public-aircraft mission a Part 107 operation.

Questions the records can help answer next

  • What are the final as-built dock coordinates, and how were the survey's Class D description and current FAA footprint reconciled?
  • What measurement supports the Talking Paper's 90–95% coverage estimate?
  • Are CAD, Flock or duress launch rules enabled, and what remote-pilot decision and authorization checks occur before takeoff?
  • What written CAPS/CADZ approvals, amendments and incorporated application define operations in zero-foot cells or above grid values?
  • How do the configured outbound, return-home and contingency routes enforce the applicable altitude and airspace limits?

The geometry and records identify questions for documentation. They do not, by themselves, establish unauthorized flight or misconduct.

Reproducible method and snapshot limits

Downloaded geometries were projected to EPSG:5070 equal-area metres. Census water was unioned and subtracted from the city polygon. Polygon unions were intersected with land; lowest CEILING receives priority if categories overlap. Surface polygons require both a zero lower value and SFC lower code, avoiding the mistaken inclusion of elevated E5 floors. LRF D/E2 duplicates count once. The geodesic circle uses three statute miles, sampled every degree of azimuth. Presentation values are rounded; machine-readable results preserve precision.
