What the analysis found

25consecutive detections 20 minutes or less apart
10possible same-outing connections 20–90 minutes apart
20same-day connections more than 90 minutes apart
6same-camera burst rows collapsed as likely duplicates
Most repeated near-term sequence. Highway 107 northbound near North Hills to Kellogg Acres Road northbound at the city limits appeared 20 times across 16 dates. The elapsed times ranged from about 5.8 to 19.4 minutes, with a median of about 6.9 minutes. This establishes a repeated sequence of detections—not the road traveled between them.

Method

1 • Order

Sort documented detections

The complete 80-row export was ordered by timestamp and assigned a standardized camera label and location.

2 • Deduplicate

Collapse likely camera bursts

Repeated rows at the same camera within 60 seconds were treated as one detection event. Six rows were collapsed, leaving 74 events.

3 • Connect

Compare consecutive events

Consecutive detections on the same date were connected and grouped by elapsed time: 20 minutes or less, 20–90 minutes, or more than 90 minutes.

4 • Protect

Remove the plate

The plate is not published. The map and workbook identify the subject only as “Vehicle A.” The source CSV is not included in this public release.

Interpretation limits

Do not read the lines as verified driving routes. Each detection establishes only that the camera system associated a vehicle with a location and time. Straight connectors show chronology, not turn-by-turn travel. The data does not establish the driver, purpose of travel, stops, roads used, continuous location, or whether every detection was accurate. Vehicle make labels also varied in the source records.

The visualization is a reproducible analytical aid, not a finding of wrongdoing. Its purpose is to show what location-and-time records can permit an observer to infer—and where those inferences must stop.
