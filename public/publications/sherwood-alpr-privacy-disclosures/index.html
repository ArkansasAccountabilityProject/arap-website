Sherwood’s Written Privacy Policy vs. Two Documented Data Releases

A public-record comparison of Policy 11.08.00, CITY-2026-112, and CITY-2026-140

This is not a publication arguing that “Flock is bad.” It presents a documentation and accountability question:

Did Sherwood follow its own privacy policy when releasing ALPR records?

Sherwood Police Department Policy 11.08.00 became effective March 24, 2026—before both releases discussed below.

What the written policy says

The policy states that:

  • ALPR data may be shared only with authorized law-enforcement agencies, prosecutors, or courts through legal process.
  • Captured plate data shall not be shared for non-law-enforcement purposes.
  • Disclosure must comply with Arkansas Code §§ 12-12-1801 through 12-12-1808 and the Arkansas Freedom of Information Act.
  • Supervisors must ensure compliance and investigate misuse.
  • Department audits must examine data security and policy compliance.

The public screenshots below reproduce the relevant policy pages so readers can compare the written requirements with the documented releases.

The first release — CITY-2026-112

On July 1, 2026, Sherwood supplied records concerning one specifically identified vehicle.

The request did not claim that the requester owned the vehicle, had the owner’s consent, represented the owner, or possessed another legal interest in it.

The portal displayed 85 responsive files. The production included numerous unredacted vehicle and plate images and an 80-row spreadsheet containing:

  • the license-plate number;
  • dates and precise times;
  • camera locations;
  • direction of travel;
  • vehicle descriptions; and
  • geographic coordinates.

Those fields allowed repeated detections of the same vehicle to be organized across several weeks. ARAP’s public demonstration removes the plate and uses “Vehicle A” throughout.

During the July 27 City Council meeting, Capt. Scott Hicks acknowledged that a disclosure “did happen as a mistake once” and said proof of ownership would be required going forward.

The second release — CITY-2026-140

Seventeen days after that public statement, Sherwood supplied another ALPR production on August 13.

This request expressly stated:

“This request does not seek license-plate numbers, vehicle images, names, addresses, or other personal identifying information.”

It also specifically authorized Sherwood to redact plate numbers while preserving non-identifying statistical and outcome information.

Despite that language, Outcomes_August_13_2026 (1).csv contained exactly 10 individual license-plate values, along with dispositions, offenses, case information, and other outcome data.

The public excerpt below shows all ten source rows with every plate value removed. The original CSV is not included in this website package.

Why the distinction matters

Arkansas law distinguishes public aggregate reporting statistics from individual captured plate data.

The public is entitled to review the required statistical compilations. That does not automatically mean individual plate numbers, vehicle images, locations, and movement records should be included in a public production.

The available documentation does not establish intentional misconduct or make a final legal determination. It documents two release-process failures:

  1. A disclosure made without a documented determination that the requester was entitled to receive one vehicle’s records.
  2. A later disclosure containing 10 plate numbers that the requester expressly said were not being requested and could be redacted.

The emails identify Capt. Hicks as the person who supplied both sets of responsive files internally to the FOIA coordinator. The available records do not identify who reviewed, approved, uploaded, or completed the external release at every stage.

The accountability question

When a city has a written privacy policy, statutory disclosure restrictions, and a publicly announced corrective procedure—but identifiable plate information is released again—who should be held responsible?

  • The employee preparing the records?
  • The supervisor reviewing them?
  • The records custodian releasing them?
  • The Police Chief responsible for policy compliance?
  • The City Attorney responsible for legal guidance?
  • The Mayor and City Council responsible for city-level oversight?

At what level of city government should responsibility for a repeated breakdown ultimately rest?
